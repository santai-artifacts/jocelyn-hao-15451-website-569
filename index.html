The Course

Course Description

15-451/651 is an advanced undergraduate and masters-level course on the design and analysis of algorithms. We study the modeling techniques, algorithmic paradigms, and analysis methods that let us solve computational problems efficiently — and prove that our solutions are correct and fast.

Topics span linear-time selection and sorting lower bounds, randomization and hashing, amortized analysis and advanced data structures, dynamic programming, network flows, game theory and linear programming, online and approximation algorithms, computational geometry, and algebraic methods including the Fast Fourier Transform and gradient descent.

Prerequisites. A minimum grade of C in 15-210, 21-241, and 15-251 (or 21-228). You should be comfortable writing proofs and reasoning about asymptotic running time.

Recitations

Recitations meet on Fridays and come in two flavors. You may switch between sections and styles at any time — attend whichever suits how you like to learn.

Review-heavy

Spend extra time on definitions and key ideas before working problems. A gentler pace — may not get through every problem.

Problem-heavy

Dive straight into the problems at a faster pace. Assumes you have reviewed the material beforehand.

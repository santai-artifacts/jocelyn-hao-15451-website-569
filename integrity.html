Academic Integrity

Collaborate to learn — but the work you submit must be your own.

Learning algorithms is a collaborative, social activity, and we want you to discuss ideas with your classmates. At the same time, the understanding you build only counts if the work you turn in genuinely reflects your own thinking. This page explains where the line falls.

Encouraged

  • Discussing problems, ideas, and approaches with classmates at the whiteboard.
  • Talking through concepts from lecture, recitation, and the notes.
  • Asking questions on Ed and at office hours.
  • Using the posted lecture notes and standard textbooks as references.

Not allowed

  • Sharing, copying, or looking at another student's written solutions.
  • Searching for or using solutions from the internet or past semesters.
  • Using AI tools to generate solutions you then submit as your own.
  • Any form of unauthorized aid during exams.

The Rule

Write Independently, Cite Everything

The guiding principle is simple: you may discuss problems, but you must write up every solution entirely on your own, from your own understanding. A good test — the “empty room” rule: after discussing a problem, discard all notes from that discussion, wait an hour, and reconstruct the solution alone. What you write should come from your head, not from a page you are transcribing.

Always cite your collaboration

  • List the people you discussed each problem with at the top of your submission.
  • Cite any book, paper, or online resource you consulted.
  • Citing collaboration is not a penalty — it is exactly what honest work looks like.
Consequences. Violations are taken seriously and are reported to the university in accordance with the CMU Academic Integrity Policy. Penalties range from a failing grade on the assignment to failing the course. When in doubt, ask an instructor before you submit.
